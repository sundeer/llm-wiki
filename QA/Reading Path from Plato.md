## Question

I've just finished a lecture series on Plato's Republic. Seems like a good starting place to understand this topic in more depth. Give me some ideas for what to read next.

## Answer

You've finished at the best possible starting point. The wiki's core thesis about the MAGA New Right rests on a chain of transmission that runs almost directly from the *Republic*. Here is a reading path, organized by how closely each work connects to what you've just read.

---

### Tier 1: The Decisive Mediator — Leo Strauss

Before touching anything explicitly about the New Right, read **Leo Strauss**. [[Leo Strauss]] is the figure who made Plato's *Republic* the center of modern American political philosophy. Everything in this wiki that connects back to Plato runs through him.

**Start with:**

- **Leo Strauss, *Natural Right and History* (1953)** — Strauss's argument that there are knowable standards of justice transcending history — a secularization of Plato's Form of the Good (Republic VI-VII). This is the philosophical backbone of [[West Coast Straussianism]] and the conceptual foundation for the Claremonters' claim that they know what America's founding *truly* means. Chapter II ("Natural Right and the Historical Approach") is the clearest entry point.

- **Leo Strauss, *Persecution and the Art of Writing* (1952)** — develops the doctrine of [[Esotericism]]: great philosophers write in two registers, concealing heterodox teachings from the many while revealing them to careful readers. This is how Strauss reads the *Republic* itself (the Noble Lie, guardian communism, and philosopher-kings are not prescriptions but demonstrations of the *impossibility* of the ideal city). It is also the intellectual framework behind [[Michael Anton]]'s pseudonymous *Flight 93 Election* and [[Curtis Yarvin]]'s deliberately dense prose.

The *Republic*-to-Strauss connection is exact: Strauss's "natural right" is a secularized Form of the Good; his "best regime" is a secularized philosopher-king; his "esotericism" is a reading method developed specifically on Platonic texts.

---

### Tier 2: The Popularizer — Allan Bloom

**[[Allan Bloom]], *The Closing of the American Mind* (1987)**

The most accessible and culturally influential translation of Straussian ideas. Bloom applies *Republic* Books VIII–IX — the democratic degeneration sequence — to American higher education. His argument: American students arrive at university already democratic souls in Plato's sense — all pleasures declared equal, no claim that some ways of life are genuinely better than others. This relativism (which Bloom traces to the absorption of Nietzsche and Heidegger into American social science) is the soul's *closing*, not its opening.

The parallels to Plato are explicit throughout. Bloom's indictment of rock music, of campus relativism, and of the collapse of the humanities maps almost point-by-point onto Plato's treatment of democratic culture in Book VIII ("insolence called good breeding; anarchy, freedom; extravagance, magnificence; shamelessness, courage," 560d–561a).

The book sold over a million copies and gave the conservative intellectual movement a philosophical vocabulary it still uses. It is also where [[Michael Anton]] was intellectually formed — Anton was Bloom's student, and *The Flight 93 Election* is in many ways Bloom's diagnosis converted into electoral catastrophism.

---

### Tier 3: The Contemporary Application — Patrick Deneen

**[[Patrick Deneen]], *Why Liberalism Failed* (2018)**

Deneen is the most important contemporary thinker in this wiki for understanding how the *Republic*'s democratic degeneration argument has been translated into a live political program. His thesis: liberalism is not failing *despite* its success but *because* of it. The very logic of liberal individualism — unlimited freedom, radical autonomy, the dissolution of all inherited hierarchies and traditions — destroys the communities that make human flourishing possible.

This is *Republic* Book VIII restated for a contemporary audience. Plato's sequence (unlimited freedom → everyone's pleasure declared equal → the soul becomes a slave to its largest appetite → tyranny) is Deneen's sequence (liberal autonomy → atomization → monopoly capitalism and administrative dependency → soft despotism). The philosophical architecture is the same.

Obama listed the book among his notable reads of 2018 — a sign of how effectively Deneen framed a critique legible across partisan lines. Within the New Right, it became the intellectual permission structure for abandoning liberal commitments: if liberalism has already failed on its own terms, there is no reason to defend its procedures.

---

### Tier 4: The Wiki's Anchor Text

**[[Laura K. Field]], *Furious Minds* (2025)**

Field's book is the wiki's primary source and the most direct guide to how all of the above — Plato, Strauss, Bloom, Deneen — flows into the contemporary MAGA New Right. She traces the intellectual lineage explicitly, showing how Straussian reading habits, Bloomian cultural criticism, and postliberal political theory converged with paleoconservative populism and online Hard Right radicalism to produce the movement.

Field is also a sharp critic rather than merely a describer. She identifies how Straussian esotericism creates an intellectual culture hospitable to the Noble Lie in practice — an elite that believes it understands the founding better than the democratic majority, and that this superior understanding licenses countermajoritarian action.

### Short Reads (Free Online)

If you want to test the water before committing to books:

1. **[[The Flight 93 Election]]** (Michael Anton / "Publius Decius Mus," *Claremont Review of Books*, 2016) — the essay that catalyzed the New Right's MAGA turn. It is 3,500 words and applies Platonic catastrophism (democratic degeneration → last exit before tyranny) to the 2016 election. [[Rush Limbaugh]] amplified it to mass audience the day after publication. The explicit pseudonym "Publius Decius Mus" is itself an act of Straussian esotericism.

2. **[[Glenn Ellmers]]'s "The People Are Not the Country"** (*American Mind*, 2021) — 2,000 words. The Cave allegory applied to American politics: "real Americans" (Trump voters) have escaped the cave; the rest are prisoners who mistake shadows for reality.

---

### The Route You Are Taking

Read in this order, you are tracing the exact transmission chain the wiki maps:

**Plato's *Republic*** → Strauss's *Natural Right and History* + *Persecution and the Art of Writing* → Bloom's *Closing of the American Mind* → Deneen's *Why Liberalism Failed* → Anton's *Flight 93 Election* → Field's *Furious Minds*

Each step is a translation: from philosophical argument to American political application, and finally to the synthesis that explains what the New Right actually is and why it has the intellectual form it does.
