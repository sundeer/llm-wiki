# Fusionism (Reagan-Buckley Conservatism)

The dominant intellectual framework of American conservatism from roughly the 1950s through 2016 — the establishment that the [[MAGA New Right]] explicitly revolted against.

Also called: establishment conservatism, movement conservatism, the Reagan coalition.

---

## What It Was

"Fusionism" describes the coalition that Frank Meyer theorized in the 1950s and that Ronald Reagan embodied politically: a fusion of social conservatism, free-market economics, and anticommunism (Reagan's "three-legged stool" or "Gipper's Stool").

**The three legs:**
1. **Social conservatism** — traditional values, family, religion, law and order
2. **Free-market economics (fiscal conservatism)** — low taxes, deregulation, free trade, laissez-faire
3. **Anticommunism** — hawkish foreign policy, NATO, interventionism, strong military

The key ideological node was *National Review*, founded by William F. Buckley in 1955, which served as the intellectual anchor of the American right for many decades. Buckley's aim was to "articulate a position on world affairs which a conservative candidate can adhere to without fear of intellectual embarrassment or political surrealism."

---

## Key Figures of Fusionism

- **William F. Buckley** (1925–2008) — founder of *National Review*; the movement's intellectual godfather
- **Frank Meyer** — theorist of fusionism; argued traditional values, liberal economics, and anticommunism were all essential to freedom
- **James Burnham** — editor at *National Review*; hard anticommunist
- **Reagan** — the political embodiment of the fusionist coalition
- **Heritage Foundation, American Enterprise Institute** — the institutional infrastructure

---

## Why the New Right Revolted Against It

The New Right's primary case against fusionism is that it **failed**. [[Michael Anton]]'s [[The Flight 93 Election]] is the most famous statement of this charge: for all their think-tanks, conferences, and policy journals, conservatives have been "losing ground for at least a century." Fusionism was, in Anton's phrase, a "Washington Generals" operation — showing up to lose while being paid.

More specifically, the New Right objects to fusionism on four fronts:

1. **Immigration:** Fusionists accepted (or enabled) mass immigration; the New Right sees this as demographic suicide
2. **Trade:** Fusionist free-trade dogma destroyed American manufacturing; economic nationalism is the New Right alternative
3. **Foreign policy:** Neoconservative interventionism (Iraq, Afghanistan) was catastrophic; the New Right wants isolationism
4. **Social issues:** Fusionism was too accommodating of progressive cultural change; the New Right wants to use state power to enforce traditionalist social values

---

## The New Right's Four-Legged Stool

Where Reagan had three legs, the New Right has four:
1. Social conservativism (but with state enforcement, not just cultural persuasion)
2. Economic nationalism (replacing free-market economics)
3. Isolationist foreign policy (replacing anticommunism/interventionism)
4. Anti-immigration (new addition)
