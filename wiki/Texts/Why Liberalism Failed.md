# Why Liberalism Failed

**Book by [[Patrick Deneen]]; Princeton University Press, 2018; the foundational diagnosis of the [[Postliberalism|postliberal]] movement; argues that liberalism is failing not despite its success but because of it**

---

## The Argument

*Why Liberalism Failed* makes a paradoxical claim: liberalism is not failing because it has been defeated or because of external pressures. It is failing because it *succeeded*. The very achievements of liberal ideology — individual autonomy, market freedom, administrative rationality — have corroded the pre-liberal communities, traditions, and practices that made human flourishing possible.

Deneen identifies three areas where liberal success produces liberal failure:

1. **Culture and family**: Liberal individualism has atomized families, communities, and religious traditions. By treating the individual as the primary unit of social life, liberalism has dissolved the inherited forms of association that give individuals meaning and support.

2. **Economics**: Market liberalism has produced monopoly capitalism, economic insecurity, and the destruction of working-class communities. The "free market" turns out to be heavily dependent on state power and generates massive inequality.

3. **Education**: Liberal universities, committed to critical individualism and the liberation of each student from inherited traditions, have produced graduates who know how to critique everything and affirm nothing — leaving them without the intellectual and moral resources for genuine human flourishing.

---

## The Diagnosis vs. Prescription Problem

*Why Liberalism Failed* is stronger as diagnosis than as prescription. Deneen's proposed alternative — a return to localist, communitarian, religious forms of life — is vague and arguably nostalgic. [[Adrian Vermeule]] criticizes Deneen's localism explicitly in [[Integration from Within]]: the administrative state exists and cannot simply be bypassed by small communities; the question is who controls it.

In his later book *Regime Change* (2023), Deneen became more explicit about the need for confrontational politics to achieve postliberal ends — moving closer to the Vermeulean position on state power.

---

## Reception and Significance

*Why Liberalism Failed* was reviewed approvingly by figures across the political spectrum — including Barack Obama, who listed it among his notable readings of 2018. This cross-partisan reception reflects both the book's genuine intellectual quality and the vagueness of its prescriptions: liberals and conservatives could both agree something is wrong; the fight is over what to do.

Within the New Right, it became a foundational text — the intellectual permission structure for abandoning liberal commitments. If liberalism has already failed on its own terms, there is no need to mourn its passing or defend its procedures.

[[Furious Minds|Field]] notes that Deneen's critique, despite its genuine insights, is easily weaponized for purposes he may not endorse: the same argument structure that diagnoses liberalism's failures can justify almost any authoritarian alternative as preferable to the status quo.
