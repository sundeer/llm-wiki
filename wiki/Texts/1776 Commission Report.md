# 1776 Commission Report

**Official Trump administration document; published January 18, 2021 (two days before Trump left office); produced by a commission of 18 conservative academics, politicians, and activists (no professional historians); direct response to the *New York Times* 1619 Project; West Coast Straussian framework; omits slavery from its first 10 pages on the founding**

---

## What It Is

The 1776 Commission Report is the official output of Trump's **President's Advisory 1776 Commission**, convened in 2020 as a direct counter to *The New York Times*' 1619 Project. The 1619 Project had argued that slavery and the experiences of Black Americans should be understood as foundational to American history; the 1776 Report argued that the proper frame was the Declaration of Independence's natural-rights ideals, and that patriotic education required celebrating the Founding rather than interrogating it.

Biden dissolved the commission on his first day in office (January 20, 2021). The report was removed from the White House website — but not before being downloaded millions of times and widely circulated.

---

## The Commission

The commission was composed of **18 members**: conservative academics, politicians, and activists — notably, no professional historians of the American founding or of slavery. Co-chairs:
- **Larry P. Arnn** (Hillsdale College president; West Coast Straussian; Jaffa student)
- **Carol Swain** (Vanderbilt political scientist; conservative Black intellectual)

Executive director: **Matthew Spalding** (director of Hillsdale's DC Kirby Center; student of Charles Kesler; West Coast Straussian).

Also on the commission: **Harry V. Jaffa** (Arnn's mentor; architect of West Coast Straussianism; in one of his final public acts).

The commission met **twice**: January 5 and January 15, 2021 — and produced its report within three days of its second meeting.

---

## Core Argument

The Report argued that America's founding documents — especially the Declaration of Independence — established a framework of universal natural rights that, properly understood, contained within them the principles for resolving all subsequent injustices (including slavery). The purpose of patriotic education was to teach this founding framework as the source of American greatness.

Following Jaffa's West Coast Straussian line: the Declaration's "all men are created equal" was the real America, and all American history is best understood as a project of progressively realizing that ideal.

---

## The "Challenges" Section

The report's third substantive section is titled "Challenges to America's Principles." The challenges enumerated: slavery, progressivism, fascism, communism, and "racism and identity politics." Field: "this is an unserious list; the inclusion of progressivism and identity politics is a sign of the commissioners' extraordinary and unthinking partisanship." Even Charles Kesler later expressed regret about how progressivism was treated in the final report.

**The Calhoun comparison**: The report's identity politics section reaches peak sophistry by likening contemporary affirmative action to the thought of John C. Calhoun — claiming that post-Civil Rights identity politics represented "group rights" "not unlike those advanced by Calhoun and his followers." Calhoun was famous for his elaborate defenses of the white majorities within the slaveholding South. The report thus equates those who fought *against* inequality with those who fought *for* Southern white people's right to hold slaves (which Calhoun deemed a "positive good" for both enslavers and enslaved). Field: "When precisely did Black people and other minorities gain so much power? And if such a shift happened, why are inequalities still so pronounced?"

**The appendix**: Even more extreme, the report's appendix claims: "While not as barbaric or dehumanizing, this new creed creates new *hierarchies as unjust as the old hierarchies of the antebellum South*, making a mockery of equality with an ever-changing scale of special privileges on the basis of racial and sexual identities."

## Field's Critique

**The opening problem**: The first 10 pages of the report cover the Declaration and Constitution at length without mentioning slavery. This is not incidental. West Coast Straussianism tends to argue that the Founders' ideals transcend their historical failures — but the practical effect of leading with 10 pages of "all men are created equal" without acknowledging chattel slavery is to tell a founding story in which slavery is at most a deviation, never a constitutive feature.

**"Highly jingoistic" tone**: Field describes the report's tone as suffused with uncritical celebration. The problem is not that it argues for the Founders' natural-rights ideals — it is that those ideals are presented without the historical friction that would make them meaningful.

**Gauzy abstractions**: Field's phrase: the report's "gauzy abstractions point away from historical realities." The Declaration's claims are invoked as self-sufficient without the material history that shaped, constrained, and often mocked them.

**The real vs. the fake problem**: Field's deeper argument is that the New Right correctly identifies a real problem (American education has sometimes tilted too far toward critique at the expense of civic formation) but prescribes a fake solution (mandating celebratory narratives is not education, it is indoctrination). She cites:
- **Rita Felski**, *The Limits of Critique*: a reminder that reflexive critique is not always edifying; education can productively orient students toward wonder and appreciation, not only suspicion
- **Roosevelt Montás**, *Rescuing Socrates*: a defense of great-books liberal education written from the experience of an immigrant student for whom those texts were genuinely transformative

The distinction Field draws: a great liberal arts education (including the texts the New Right claims to champion) produces students capable of independent judgment and genuine civic engagement — not students who have absorbed approved conclusions about national greatness.
