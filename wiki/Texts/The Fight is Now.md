# The Fight is Now

**Authors:** Ryan P. Williams, Arthur Milikh, Matthew J. Peterson, James Poulos, and American Mind Editors  
**Published:** November 4, 2020 — the day after the 2020 election  
**Published in:** *The American Mind* (Claremont Institute publication)

---

## What It Is

A statement published by the leadership of the [[Claremont Institute]] on the morning after the 2020 election, before votes were fully counted, calling on Republicans to fight the election results. It is a primary document in the history of the "Stop the Steal" movement and a direct precursor to January 6, 2021.

**Signatories:**
- Ryan P. Williams — President, Claremont Institute
- Arthur Milikh — Executive Director, Center for the American Way of Life
- Matthew J. Peterson — VP of Education, Claremont Institute; Founding Editor, The American Mind
- James Poulos — Executive Editor, The American Mind

---

## Core Argument

The essay makes three claims:

1. **The Democrats stole the election** — "the Democratic Party just broke all the norms related to voting in America." The statement cites lawsuits, COVID-related rule changes, and mail ballot counting delays as evidence of deliberate fraud. It links to reports from Detroit and Philadelphia as "troubling irregularities."

2. **Republicans must fight legally and publicly** — "Swarms of lawyers must push for transparency in all the Democratic city machines now churning out votes for Biden." The FBI should investigate. Recounts must be demanded.

3. **Republicans must take to the streets** — "President Trump should plan several more rallies. *Huge ones*." The essay explicitly calls for rallies and protests against what it characterizes as an illegitimate count. "Weak sisters on the right must be called out."

The essay ends with a call for psychological resilience: "In military doctrine, psychological operations only work on a populace that is already experiencing a defeat."

---

## Significance

- Published just hours after polls closed, before any credible claim of fraud had been investigated — demonstrating that the "Stop the Steal" narrative was pre-planned, not evidence-driven
- Came from the institutional center of the Claremonter movement, not its fringe
- Directly echoes the catastrophism and emergency politics of [[The Flight 93 Election]] — the same "charge the cockpit" logic applied to the post-election period
- The Claremont Institute's institutional imprimatur gave the Stop the Steal narrative intellectual legitimacy within conservative circles
- The calls for "huge rallies" anticipate the January 6 rally at the Ellipse

**Field's framing:** This document is evidence that the New Right "fully cohered" only after January 6 — but also that the road to January 6 was paved by institutions like the Claremont Institute that legitimized the stolen election narrative immediately after November 3.

---

## Contradictions

- The essay decries Democratic "norm-breaking" around election procedures while itself breaking the norm of accepting vote counts before they are complete
- It invokes legal process ("bring out the lawyers") while simultaneously calling for extra-legal street pressure ("bring out the people")
- The authors hold PhD-level credentials in political philosophy but the essay relies on innuendo and links to partisan media rather than evidence
