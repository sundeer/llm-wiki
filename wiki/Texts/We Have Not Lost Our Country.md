# We Have Not Lost Our Country

**[[Rush Limbaugh]], *The Rush Limbaugh Show*, broadcast monologue, November 9, 2016 — the morning-after-Trump's-victory show; the Limbaugh-narrated counterpart to [[The Shaming of the Never Trumpers]] (September 7), and the explicit talk-radio framing of the 2016 outcome as (a) ideological, (b) Brexit-twin, and (c) repudiation of the Democratic identity-group politics.**

---

## Why It Matters Here

Three things make this transcript wiki-relevant:

1. **"Big Government was told to take a hike last night just like Big Government was told to take a hike in the Brexit vote."** This is the canonical Limbaugh statement of the **Brexit-Trump twin-event frame** that becomes a load-bearing piece of the post-2016 Hard-Right transatlantic imagination ([[Steve Bannon]], [[Yoram Hazony]]'s NatCon, [[Viktor Orbán]] reception). The frame predates and shapes [[National Conservatism]]'s self-understanding.
2. **"It is always ideological."** Limbaugh's explicit rejection of the "this isn't ideological, it's economic anxiety" media frame — a frame later echoed across academic literature as the dominant interpretation Limbaugh's audience was *already* contesting in real time. The wiki uses this as evidence that the New Right *self-understood* its 2016 win as ideological from day one.
3. **The "groups vs. citizens" reframe.** Limbaugh: "all you need to do is approach the American people as citizens, as the American people. To hell with what group they're in." The structural argument that the Democratic Party's identity-group politics *is itself* what was repudiated — ahead of the more academic versions of this claim from postliberals (e.g., [[Patrick Deneen]] on "atomization"; [[R.R. Reno]]'s "strong gods").

---

## Core Claims

- **The election as repudiation, not normal alternation.** "Total repudiation of Big Government… of the Obama presidency… of the Democrat Party by the people I always describe as those who make this country work."
- **The GOP establishment's defeatism.** "I've had Mitt Romney come to my studio. I've had Rand Paul come to my studio. I've had Republican presidential candidate after candidate… they all told me, 'We can't win, Rush, with Republican votes alone.'" Trump proved them wrong. Limbaugh's running thesis since at least 2008: the GOP's establishment had internalized the impossibility of winning ideologically.
- **The "voice in this country" thesis.** "Donald Trump heard a voice out in this country that no one else heard… A lot of people are that voice! People didn't have to hear it; we have been shouting it!" Limbaugh's claim of authorship: talk radio (and his audience) *constituted* the voice Trump merely heard.
- **The mandate framing.** "We hold both. So now we have the House, and now we have the Senate, and now we have the White House — and now we have no excuses. There aren't any excuses anymore." This is the *unified-government* version of Anton's "charge the cockpit" — having charged it, no further excuse for fusionist gradualism.
- **The Hillary group-list moment.** Limbaugh sneered at Hillary's concession-speech invocation of "LGBT, women, [etc.]": "Groups. Victims. The way they see the country is not the way this country is. This is not a nation of victims!" The argument fuses with [[The Shaming of the Never Trumpers|Shaming]]'s anti-establishment thread: identity-group politics is the elite's instrument *and* the deformation of citizenship.

---

## The Brexit-Trump Frame

The Brexit reference is the wiki-significant move: Limbaugh treats Brexit and Trump as *the same political phenomenon* — "Big Government" repudiation in its English-speaking-democracy variant. This is the popular-conservative version of the frame [[Yoram Hazony]] systematizes in [[The Virtue of Nationalism]] (2018) and [[Steve Bannon]] operationalizes in his European-populist-network strategy (2017–onward). Limbaugh stating it on November 9, 2016, six weeks after [[Steve Bannon|Bannon]] became Trump's campaign chief executive, is evidence that the frame was *already in popular circulation* before the institutionalization.

---

## Wiki Reading

Together with [[The Shaming of the Never Trumpers]], this transcript completes the talk-radio frame of 2016. *Shaming* (September 7) is the *call-to-arms* — addressed to conservative intellectuals refusing to vote Trump. *Lost Our Country* (November 9) is the *post-victory consolidation* — addressed to the GOP establishment refusing to take ideological yes for an answer. Together they document that the Trump base understood the 2016 election as **ideological, civilizational, and transatlantic** in real time, not retroactively.
