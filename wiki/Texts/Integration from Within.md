# Integration from Within

**Author:** [[Adrian Vermeule]]  
**Published:** February 2018, *American Affairs* (Vol. II, No. 1)  
**Genre:** Review essay  
**Ostensible subject:** *Why Liberalism Failed* by [[Patrick Deneen]]

---

## What It Is

A landmark essay in the intellectual history of the MAGA New Right. Published as a review of Deneen's *Why Liberalism Failed*, but Vermeule uses the review as a springboard to lay out his own, far more radical strategy for dismantling liberalism: occupying the liberal administrative state from within and turning its machinery to "postliberal" ends.

The essay was published in *American Affairs*, the journal co-founded by [[Julius Krein]] and [[Gladden Pappin]], which emerged from the [[Journal of American Greatness]] orbit.

---

## Argument

**On Deneen:** Vermeule praises Deneen's diagnosis — liberalism has been undone by its own success, hollowing out the very institutions (church, family, community) that it needed to sustain itself. But he attacks Deneen's prescription: the "Benedict Option" of retreating into localist communities. Vermeule's objection is strategic: liberal agents have a comprehensive theory of the good and will not ultimately allow nonliberal communities to exist. Localism is a holding pattern under permanent existential threat.

**The alternate ending:** Vermeule proposes what he calls "integration from within." Rather than retreating, nonliberal actors should:

> "Strategically locate themselves within liberal institutions and work to undo the liberalism of the state from within."

His models are biblical: Joseph, Mordecai, Esther, and Daniel — people who operated loyally within hostile imperial structures while preserving and ultimately advancing their own faith community. And more radically: St. Paul and St. Cecilia, who sought not merely to survive but to transform the empire from within.

**The key operational vision:**
> "These agents may even come to occupy the commanding heights of the administrative state... They may have a great deal of discretion to further human dignity and the common good, defined entirely in substantive rather than procedural-technical terms."

**The goal:** to turn the vast liberal administrative state — "the great instrument" liberalism has built — toward "a new and doubtless very different future." In other words: seize the state and use it for integralist ends.

**On coercion vs. persuasion:** Vermeule waves away the distinction, quoting James Fitzjames Stephen: "the weaker opinion... is rooted out to the last fiber, the place where it grew being seared as with a hot iron." The essay endorses "soft paternalism" and "nudging" as modes of reshaping populations — but its logic clearly extends further.

---

## Significance

This essay is a blueprint for what became known as "Common Good Constitutionalism" and is arguably the founding document of Catholic integralism as a practical political strategy (rather than just a theological position). It influenced Vermeule's later book *Common Good Constitutionalism* (2022).

The strategy it describes — occupying the administrative state and using it for illiberal ends — is visible in Project 2025 and the second Trump administration's approach to the federal bureaucracy.

**Field's assessment:** Vermeule represents the "most sophisticated" thinkers of the New Right, and also the most "sincerely committed" to populist economic reform. But his program is a "constitutional defense of far-right conservative authoritarianism" dressed in Catholic natural law language.

---

## Key Concepts Introduced

- **Integration from Within** — the strategy of occupying liberal institutions rather than building alternatives
- **Liberalism as a "fighting faith"** — Vermeule's framing of liberalism as a world religion with its own clergy, sacraments, and eschatology
- **The gravity well of liberalism** — how even critics of liberalism get pulled back into its assumptions (his critique of Deneen's localism)
- **"It takes a comprehensive theory to beat a comprehensive theory"** — the core strategic argument

---

## Tensions and Contradictions

- Vermeule appeals to democratic legitimacy and "service to the regime" while describing a strategy of using state power to impose a substantive vision of the good on a population that has not consented to it
- The biblical models (Joseph, Esther, Daniel) were *minorities* surviving in hostile empires — Vermeule is appropriating their strategy for a movement that seeks majority domination
- The essay was written in 2018, before Vermeule became fully associated with MAGA; by 2024 its prescriptions looked less like political theory and more like a literal playbook
