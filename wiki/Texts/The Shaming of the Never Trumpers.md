# The Shaming of the Never Trumpers

**[[Rush Limbaugh]], *The Rush Limbaugh Show*, broadcast monologue, September 7, 2016 — the day after [[Michael Anton|"Publius Decius Mus"]] published [[The Flight 93 Election]] at the *[[Claremont Review of Books]]*; the *single broadcast* that moved Anton's catastrophist Claremont essay from intellectual-Right specialist circulation to mass conservative audience.**

---

## Why It Matters Here

This transcript is the wiki's primary documentary evidence for a load-bearing claim about the 2016 election: **the [[Claremont Institute]] → Limbaugh → mass-MAGA pipeline operated, in real time, on September 7, 2016**. Limbaugh devoted most of an episode to reading and amplifying Anton's piece. He explicitly told listeners "you really should read it" and noted he'd crashed the Claremont site in the first half hour by promoting it. This is the moment Anton's argument broke containment.

It also documents two *anterior* moments in the same pipeline:

1. Limbaugh credits himself with coining "Washington Generals" before Anton used the term. Anton's borrowing — confirmed in the transcript — is the smaller-scale tell that the talk-radio register and the Claremont register were already convergent before the *Flight 93* publication.
2. Limbaugh names ["Dr. Angelo Codevilla's ruling class versus country class piece from the *American Spectator*"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelo_Codevilla) (July 2010) as the prior text Anton's *Flight 93* most resembles. The Codevilla → Anton → Limbaugh→Trump-base lineage is the most concise *intellectual genealogy* of the Trump-era populist conservative argument.

---

## What Limbaugh Read Aloud

Limbaugh read out roughly half of Anton's piece on air, paragraph by paragraph, with commentary. The selections — and Limbaugh's interpretive frame — are themselves a primary source for the *popular* version of the [[Flight 93 Election]] argument:

- **"Charge the cockpit or you die."** Limbaugh quoted this opening twice and treated it as the thesis.
- **"Conservatism, Inc., reeks of failure."** Limbaugh's framing: the Beltway conservative class derives its standard of living from professional opposition without ever winning. They are "the Washington Generals" — the Harlem Globetrotters' designated foil team.
- **"Are any of them truly fundamental?"** Limbaugh treated Anton's challenge to think-tank/policy conservatism as the core of the piece — *the position papers don't matter when the country is hanging in the balance*.
- **The "Davos class" passage.** Anton: "If you're a member or fellow-traveler of the Davos class, chances are [the last twenty years have been good]. If you're among the subspecies conservative intellectual or politician, you've accepted… your status on the roster of the Washington Generals." Limbaugh stopped to claim authorship of "Washington Generals."
- **The immigration passage.** Anton: "the ceaseless importation of Third World foreigners with no tradition of, taste for, or experience in liberty." Limbaugh segued into a long account of his rejection of the "Hispanics-are-natural-conservatives" sales pitch.
- **The closing.** "Trump, alone among candidates… stood up to say: I want to live. I want my party to live. I want my country to live. I want my people to live."

## Limbaugh's Commentary as Primary Source

Limbaugh's interpolations are themselves load-bearing for the wiki:

- **"Conservatism no longer applies. We're way past that."** Limbaugh's own gloss on Anton — and the cleanest single statement of the [[Counterrevolution|counterrevolutionary]] thesis Anton's piece *implicitly* makes but doesn't quite state. Limbaugh translates the Claremont Straussianism into a vernacular declaration that the movement's existing institutions are not just inadequate but *complicit*.
- **"There is no such thing as a conservative-in-waiting."** Limbaugh's rejection of the GOP-establishment "natural-conservative Hispanic" frame is the *exact* anti-immigrant logic Anton encodes more elliptically. The transcript is the Rosetta Stone: the *demographic-replacement* core of the *Flight 93* argument is plain in the popular register and elliptical in the elite register.
- **"They don't think they need a foil anymore."** Limbaugh's repeated trope is that the left has decided the *Washington Generals* show no longer needs to go on. This is a projection from talk-radio political-economy onto the broader political system — and the foundation, two months later, of the [[2016 Election]]'s "Big Government was told to take a hike" framing in [[We Have Not Lost Our Country]].

## The Codevilla Connection

Limbaugh: "this is on a par with Dr. Angelo Codevilla's ruling class versus country class piece from the *American Spectator* a few years ago." Codevilla's "America's Ruling Class — and the Perils of Revolution" (July 2010) is the piece Limbaugh names as the structural antecedent. This is the wiki's most economical genealogy of the Trump-era populist-conservative argument:

> Codevilla 2010 (*American Spectator*) — ruling class vs. country class
> → Anton 2016 (*Claremont Review of Books*) — *Flight 93*
> → Limbaugh September 7 (talk radio) — mass amplification
> → Trump November 2016 — political incarnation

Each step makes the Codevilla original more catastrophist and more *demographic*. By Limbaugh's reading, the elite's tolerance of immigration *is* the existential threat.

## Wiki Reading

The transcript is the documentary anchor for the wiki's claim that the Claremont-MAGA pipeline operated through *Limbaugh as transmission node* in 2016. It also documents how the *Flight 93* argument was *received* — the immigration-replacement core was front-and-center in the talk-radio version even where it was buried in Anton's prose. The wiki should treat this transcript as evidence that the elite-vs-mass register difference inside the New Right is **rhetorical, not substantive**.
