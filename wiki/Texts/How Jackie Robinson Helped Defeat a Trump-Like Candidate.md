# How Jackie Robinson Helped Defeat a Trump-Like Candidate

**Matthew Delmont, *The Atlantic*, March 19, 2016; the canonical short account of the 1964 GOP through the lens of its few remaining Black Republicans, drawing the explicit Goldwater-to-Trump line as it was being drawn in real time during the 2016 primary.**

---

## Why It Matters Here

Two functions in the wiki:

1. **Eyewitness record of the [[1964 Republican National Convention]]'s racial atmosphere.** Black GOP delegates verbally assaulted; William Young (Pennsylvania delegate) "had his suit set on fire and was told to 'keep in your own place'"; New Jersey delegate George Fleming: "They call you 'nigger,' push you and step on your feet… I had to leave to keep my self-respect" (citing Leah Wright Rigueur, *The Loneliness of the Black Republican*). This is the primary-source layer that the more analytic [[He Knew He Was Right|Menand]] and [[How the 1964 Republican Convention Sparked a Revolution From the Right|Perlstein-Smithsonian]] pieces summarize but do not narrate.
2. **Contemporaneous Goldwater→Trump diagnosis.** Delmont (March 2016) was writing during the primary, not retrospectively. The article documents that the Goldwater→Trump analogy was already operative in Black Republican memory before Trump won the nomination — making this an early node in the wiki's *Trump-as-continuity* (rather than aberration) thesis.

---

## Core Claims

- **Jackie Robinson was a stalwart Republican** who campaigned for Nixon in 1960. Goldwater's nomination shocked him: "I now believe I know how it felt to be a Jew in Hitler's Germany." Founded Republicans and Independents for Lyndon Johnson after the convention.
- **Robinson's diagnosis was structural, not partisan.** "If Goldwater has been defeated, but Goldwaterism remains triumphant in GOP councils, America faces a difficult future." This is — six decades early — the wiki's [[Counterrevolution]] thesis: the *figure* loses but the *politics* metastasize inside the party.
- **MLK's contemporaneous statement.** Goldwater's nomination was "both unfortunate and disastrous": "While not himself a racist, Mr. Goldwater articulates a philosophy which gives aid and comfort to racists. His candidacy and philosophy will serve as an umbrella under which extremists of all stripes will stand." This is the wiki's cleanest period statement of the Goldwater problem and exactly mirrors how mainstream postliberals are read against the Hard Right today.
- **The Black-Republican electoral cliff.** Eisenhower 39% (1956), Nixon 32% (1960), Goldwater 6% (1964), no Republican since above 15%. The 1964 vote was the inflection point.
- **The *Chicago Defender* archive.** Editorials called Goldwater "the most diabolical force that has ever captured the leadership of the Republican Party. After 108 years of exhortation to freedom, liberty, and justice, the GOP now becomes the label under which Fascism is oozed into the mainstream of American politics" (1964). Already deploying the F-word in the same register Field uses in [[Furious Minds]].
