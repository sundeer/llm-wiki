# The Conscience of a Conservative

**Author of record: [[Barry Goldwater]] (Victor Publishing, April 1960). Actual ghostwriter: [[L. Brent Bozell Jr.]]. Origin: a 1959 commission from a group of conservatives led by Clarence Manion (former Notre Dame Law School dean and a Bircher contributor to *National Review*) for a "pamphlet on Americanism." Outcome: 3.5 million copies sold; the most widely distributed political manifesto of postwar American conservatism; the textual canon of fusionist politics; the foundation for Goldwater's 1964 presidential run.**

---

## What It Is

A short (~120-page) declaration of conservative principle organized around three themes:

1. **Traditionalism**: man as a "spiritual animal"; conservatism as application of "the wisdom and experience and the revealed truths of the past to the problems of today"; the conservative "looks upon the enhancement of man's spiritual nature as the primary concern of political philosophy."
2. **Libertarianism**: government as a leviathan that has overgrown its constitutional role; politics as "the art of achieving the maximum amount of freedom for individuals that is consistent with the maintenance of social order"; tax limitation ("government has a right to claim an equal percentage of each man's wealth, and no more"); states' rights; anti–federal subsidy; anti–organized labor.
3. **Anti-communism**: a seven-point program "to win the Cold War," including U.S. military superiority, NATO maintenance, and encouragement of "the captive peoples" to "overthrow their captors"; central thesis that "the communists seek victories" while America seeks "settlements."

Goldwater's gloss on the project: "to apply the wisdom and experience and the revealed truths of the past to the problems of today."

---

## Why It Matters Here

*The Conscience of a Conservative* is the wiki's reference text for what fusionism *was* as a political program — the precise document that the [[MAGA New Right]] has unwound, point by point.

| *Conscience* (1960) | New Right (post-2016) |
|---|---|
| Traditionalism via private virtue & "spiritual nature" | Traditionalism via state enforcement of substantive moral ends ([[Postliberalism]]) |
| Free trade, deregulation, anti-subsidy | Economic nationalism, industrial policy ([[Oren Cass]], *American Compass*) |
| Anti-communism as foreign policy organizing principle | Anti-progressive / anti-China / anti-immigration as organizing principles |
| "Each man… responsible for his own development" | The state as agent of cultural restoration ([[Common Good Constitutionalism]]) |
| Suspicion of "concentration of power in the hands of a few men" | [[Decisionism]]: "sovereign is he who decides on the exception" |
| Constitution as instrument "for limiting the functions of government" | Constitution as instrument of substantive moral order ([[Declarationism]]) |

The book is therefore the precise foil against which to read [[Patrick Deneen]]'s *Why Liberalism Failed*, [[Adrian Vermeule]]'s [[Common Good Constitutionalism]], [[Sohrab Ahmari]]'s [[Tyranny, Inc]], and the Hazony [[National Conservatism]] Statement. Each is a deliberate refutation of one or more of *Conscience*'s pillars.

## The Bozell Authorship Problem

Goldwater authored none of the prose. Bozell — Yale-trained Catholic convert, [[William F. Buckley Jr.]]'s brother-in-law, and the same writer who, two years later, would publish his anti-fusionist 1962 essay arguing that "the story of how the free society has come to take priority over the good society is the story of the decline of the West" — wrote the actual book. The fusionist manifesto and the proto-postliberal dissent are by **the same author within a 24-month window**.

The fusionist movement therefore canonized as its central political text a book whose ghostwriter, on his own account, did not believe in fusionism's first principle (that freedom is prior to virtue). This is one of the wiki's sharper structural points: the canonical fusionist manifesto was always already shadowed by its own postliberal alternative. See [[L. Brent Bozell Jr.]] for the longer argument.
