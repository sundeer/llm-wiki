# Democracy Faces a Reactionary Counter-Mobilization

**Thomas Zimmer (Substack, "A Reflection on What We Are Up Against — Part I," 28 November 2022). Genre: short synthesis essay by a historian of postwar American conservatism. Useful here primarily for analytical vocabulary: Zimmer proposes that the [[MAGA New Right]] is best described as a "reactionary counter-mobilization against egalitarian multiracial, pluralistic democracy" — three terms each chosen against a more standard alternative ("reactionary" rather than "conservative"; "counter-mobilization" rather than "backlash"; "egalitarian multiracial, pluralistic democracy" rather than just "democracy").**

---

## The Three Terminological Moves

**1. Reactionary, not conservative.**
Zimmer joins [[Joshua Tait]] ([[Conservatives or Counterrevolutionaries?]]), the wiki's [[Counterrevolution]] page, and the broader [[Russell Kirk]]-vs.-the-movement framework: a "conservative" is attached to existing arrangements; a "reactionary" wants to *restore* an earlier arrangement that has already been overtaken. The American right is overwhelmingly the latter. The "conservative" label is rhetorical cover for a project whose substantive content is restorative, and which is therefore more accurately named "reactionary."

**2. Counter-mobilization, not backlash.**
The "backlash" frame "puts the agency solely with traditionally marginalized groups who are ultimately at fault for causing an inevitable reaction" — and is therefore attractive to anyone who wants to delegitimize "social justice" politics by treating its consequences as unavoidable. Zimmer rejects the implicit determinism. Reactionaries are *acting*: they "are deliberately participating in a political project of preventing America from ever becoming an egalitarian multiracial, pluralistic democracy." Restoring agency to the actors restores moral and political accountability. The wiki's [[Counterrevolution]] page benefits from this vocabulary — what Tait calls counterrevolution is not a passive response, it is an active mobilization.

**3. Egalitarian multiracial, pluralistic democracy, not just "democracy."**
Zimmer rejects the yes/no question of whether America is or is not "a democracy." Democracy admits of degrees; "the first question we should ask whenever someone says 'democracy' is: What kind of democracy, how much, and for whom?" Pre–1965 America was, "by contemporaneous comparison, fairly democratic if you happened to be a white Christian man — and something else entirely if you were not." The argument since 1965 has been over whether America should *complete* the egalitarian project. The reactionary right — and a much wider coalition of self-styled "moderates" — answers no.

---

## Why It Matters Here

Zimmer's frame is consonant with the wiki's existing framework but sharper in three places:

- It pairs precisely with George Thomas's [[America Is a Republic, Not a Democracy|"Republic Not a Democracy"]] piece. Thomas attacks the *constitutional pretext* the New Right uses to justify minority rule; Zimmer names the *political project* that pretext is in service of.
- It supplies the wiki's most precise short-form vocabulary for naming what the [[MAGA New Right]] is *for* (not just what it is against). The project is the prevention of a fully egalitarian, multiracial, pluralistic America — not via accident of political coalition but as positive program.
- It corrects the wiki's existing language — which, in places, still defaults to "backlash" — toward the more accurate "counter-mobilization."

---

## Limits

Zimmer's piece is short and avowedly preliminary; he promises a Part II on the *reactionary* dimension that doesn't appear to have been published in equally polished form. The piece is therefore best treated here as a vocabulary contribution rather than a fully developed framework. The wiki's longer-form analytical infrastructure remains [[Counterrevolution]] (Tait + Field), supplemented by the Zimmer terminology.
