# Journal of American Greatness

**Anonymous blog; 2015–2016; written by pseudonymous authors using ancient Roman pen names; the immediate intellectual precursor to [[American Affairs]] and [[The Flight 93 Election]]; described themselves as "Paleo-Straussians"**

---

## What It Was

The Journal of American Greatness (JAG) was an anonymous blog active in 2015–2016 during the rise of Trump's candidacy. Its authors — who used pen names from ancient Rome — were among the first to develop a sophisticated intellectual defense of Trumpism from a right-wing perspective.

Their self-designation as "Paleo-Straussians" captures their position: they drew on [[West Coast Straussianism|Straussian]] political philosophy but inflected it with [[Paleoconservatism|paleoconservative]] concerns (immigration, trade, civilizational decline). They cited authors like Sam Francis and Peter Brimelow (a white supremacist) alongside Strauss and Jaffa.

---

## Significance

JAG proved that intellectualized Trumpism could find an audience — that there was a market for high-theory defenses of populist nationalism in educated conservative circles. This discovery set the template for what the Claremonters would do post-2016.

The blog was, in [[Furious Minds|Field]]'s words, "novel for its hysterical alarmism and early flirtation with the remedy of strongman politics (or Caesarism) as a possible solution for America's ailments." This Caesarist possibility — elected strongman as emergency executive — would become a recurring theme of the New Right.

---

## The Authors

Three JAG authors eventually broke their anonymity:

- **[[Michael Anton]]** ("Publius Decius Mus") — went on to write [[The Flight 93 Election]] and serve in the Trump White House
- **[[Julius Krein]]** ("Plautus") — went on to co-found [[American Affairs]] journal
- **[[Gladden Pappin]]** — went on to become a Catholic integralist political philosopher; also co-founded [[American Affairs]]

Their divergent trajectories after JAG illustrate the range of positions available within the early Trump intellectual ecosystem.
