# Intercollegiate Studies Institute (ISI)

**Conservative college student organization; founded 1953 by Frank Chodorov (Old Right, isolationist, classically liberal); William F. Buckley as first president; campus chapters, Great Books programming, in-house publishing and journal (*Modern Age*); turned toward nationalism and Trumpism under John A. Burtka IV (president from September 2020); 2023 inaugural American Politics and Government Summit (Field attended)**

---

## What It Is

ISI is a relatively small but influential conservative organization that hosts student events, "Great Books" programming, and supports chapters and student journalists on campuses across the country. It has its own in-house publishing house and journal (*Modern Age*). The organization was inspired by William F. Buckley's *God and Man at Yale* and aimed to fill the "gaping void in higher education" — meaning the gaping void of conservatism. Buckley was its first president.

---

## The Burtka Turn (2020)

In September 2020, **John A. Burtka IV** was appointed ISI president. Burtka attended Hillsdale College for his undergraduate degree and has a graduate degree in theology from La Faculté Jean Calvin (a Protestant Reformed seminary in France). He was a Claremont Lincoln Fellow in 2018. Under Burtka's leadership, ISI added new programming and in 2022 opened a new conference center (endowed by Linda L. Bean). Some observers perceived a dramatic turn toward nationalism and Trumpism under Burtka; others contested that view.

---

## 2023 American Politics and Government Summit

In 2023, ISI hosted its inaugural **American Politics and Government Summit** at the Hilton Fort Lauderdale Marina — where Trump flags flew on the yachts docked outside. Field attended as one of the most liberal people at the conference. The atmosphere: jokes about pronouns, "the Democrat Party," 8-to-1 ratio of men to women on the program, general assumption that everyone was "on our side."

Attendees included John Eastman (his wife was a speaker), Yoram Hazony, Charles Kesler, Ryan Williams, and Michael Anton. There were also classical liberals and fusionist types in attendance. The final keynote speaker was **Robert P. George**, who gave a moderate, fusionist address that Field describes as "shadow boxing with the New Right."
