# Stephen Miller

**Senior advisor and immigration policy architect; born and raised Southern California; early protégé of David Horowitz; Duke BA in political science 2007; brought together with Richard Spencer by the Duke lacrosse case; wrote regularly for Breitbart; worked for Sen. Jeff Sessions before joining Trump campaign 2016; co-wrote "American carnage" inaugural address with Bannon; architect of travel ban targeting Muslims and child separation policy (5,000+ children); emails leaked to SPLC 2019 showing connections to white nationalist ideology and *The Camp of the Saints*; remained in White House throughout both terms**

---

## Who He Is

Stephen Miller is the central immigration policy operative of the Trump administration — the person who translated Hard Right immigration ideology into executive policy. He is not primarily an intellectual; he is an implementer. But his career illustrates how the Hard Right network — connecting Breitbart, white nationalist media, and the executive branch — functions in practice.

---

## Early Life and Formation

Born and raised in Southern California. Miller became a right-wing pundit and personality at a very young age. He was an early protégé of David Horowitz, the right-wing activist and pundit. From the start, he was opposed to immigration and multiculturalism.

Miller did his undergraduate degree at Duke University, graduating in 2007 with a degree in political science. At Duke in 2006-2007, he gained notoriety for bullheadedly defending the men's lacrosse team against rape allegations in a case that would eventually collapse. The lacrosse scandal — Field notes, "still years before Gamergate" — is cited as one episode that seeded the alt-right. It is also what brought Miller together with Richard Spencer: both were in the Duke Conservative Union, a small group led by Miller. Spencer says the lacrosse case "catalyzed me to be a pugilist for my views" — crediting Miller's example.

---

## Career

**David Horowitz / Breitbart**: Long before his formal political career, Miller had ties to the Horowitz network and wrote regularly for Breitbart News, the media operation that Bannon would later call "the platform for the alt-right."

**Jeff Sessions**: Miller worked for Alabama senator Jeff Sessions — one of the Senate's most hardline immigration restrictionists — before joining Trump's campaign in 2016.

**Trump campaign and transition**: Joined the campaign early in 2016. Was an important member of the transition team.

**Inaugural address**: Miller and Bannon reportedly co-wrote Trump's inaugural address — the "American carnage" speech: "The crime, and the gangs, and the drugs that have stolen too many lives and robbed our country of so much unrealized potential. This American carnage stops right here and stops right now."

---

## Policy Record

**Travel bans**: Miller and Bannon enacted travel bans targeting Muslims as among their first acts in the administration.

**Child separation**: Miller was the lead architect of the "zero tolerance" policy at the southern border, under which more than five thousand children were forcibly separated from their parents.

Miller served through the full four years of Trump's first term and returned for the second.

## The SPLC Email Leak (November 2019)

Hundreds of Miller's emails were leaked to the Southern Poverty Law Center's Hatewatch group by former Breitbart staffer Katie McHugh. The emails demonstrated Miller's connections not only to Breitbart but to white nationalist ideology:

- ***The Camp of the Saints*** (Jean Raspail): Miller promoted this dystopian racist novel about the "mass invasion" of France by migrants. A description of its content: migrants are presented as "a repulsive, seething mass of dark bodies"; featuring "the turd eater," described as "a black giant with his gruesomely deformed son perched atop his shoulders as a 'hideous totem'"; refugee ships filled with "indiscriminate pansexual orgies — 'a mass of hands and mouths, of phalluses and rumps' — in which even young children are fair game." Viktor Orbán has also embraced this novel.
- **Jason Richwine**: Miller promoted the work of Richwine, a Harvard PhD whose dissertation was titled "IQ and Immigration Policy." Richwine had resigned from the Heritage Foundation in 2013 after the extent of his Harvard-backed racism was exposed, but was being rehabilitated on the New Right. He and Amy Wax published an article together in *American Affairs* in Winter 2017.

When these connections were exposed in 2019, there were petitions to oust Miller. Nothing changed. On November 13, 2020, the Trump administration appointed Darren Beattie to the Commission for the Preservation of America's Heritage Abroad — and on the same day appointed Jason Richwine to a senior position at the National Institute of Standards and Technology.

## Significance

Miller's trajectory demonstrates the permeability between the Hard Right fringe and the executive branch. He contests being called "alt-right," preferring to see himself as merely adjacent — but regardless of the label, his career built the policy infrastructure for nativist immigration enforcement, and his email connections to white nationalist media (VDARE, Breitbart) demonstrate the ideological continuum.
