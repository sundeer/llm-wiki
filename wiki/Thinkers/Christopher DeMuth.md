# Christopher DeMuth

**Conservative policy intellectual; AEI president 1986–2008 (longest-serving); Reagan's "deregulation czar"; Hudson Institute distinguished fellow; Heritage Foundation 2023; chairman of National Conservatism conferences; author of "Trumpism, Nationalism, and Conservatism" (*Claremont Review of Books*, Winter 2019)**

---

## Who He Is

Christopher DeMuth is the mainstream conservative establishment figure who lent institutional legitimacy to the National Conservatism project. His involvement signaled that NatCon was not a fringe operation but a bid for mainstream conservative realignment.

A Harvard graduate who trained as a lawyer at the University of Chicago Law School, DeMuth worked in both the Nixon and Reagan administrations — he is sometimes called Reagan's "deregulation czar." He served as president of the American Enterprise Institute for over twenty years (1986–2008), then as a distinguished fellow at the Hudson Institute until moving to the Heritage Foundation in 2023.

---

## Role in National Conservatism

After [[Yoram Hazony]] created the Edmund Burke Foundation following the success of *The Virtue of Nationalism*, DeMuth became chairman of the National Conservatism conference. Field notes that "it is hard to imagine a person who could better signal National Conservatism's mainstream aspirations than DeMuth."

He was not merely a figurehead. Hazony called his Winter 2019 *Claremont Review of Books* essay — "Trumpism, Nationalism, and Conservatism" — a "watershed essay" for the movement.

---

## "Trumpism, Nationalism, and Conservatism" (2019)

DeMuth's essay opened: "Trumpism has an essence, and that essence is nationalism. It is the American version of the revival of the spirit of nationhood in the rich democracies of the North Atlantic. It is bigger than President Trump's personality and program, and is certain to outlast the drama and fate of his tenure in office."

Key positions:
- Vouched for the far-right nationalist movements of Britain, Poland, Hungary, Italy, and the "neo-nationalist parties of Germany and France" as the "spirit of nationhood" he was endorsing
- Used the "Somewhere" vs. "Anywhere" framing (from David Goodhart) to side with locals against internationalist elites
- Denied the "creedal" character of America — Bill Galston called this a "bluntly anti-Lincolnian move"

DeMuth's internal contradiction, Field notes: he recognized the need for something new and rejected whole swaths of the American political tradition going back to Lincoln, while simultaneously recommending neoliberal fiscal responsibility and failing to acknowledge how Reaganomics itself created the conditions he was decrying.

---

## The DeMuth/Orbán Interview

At the NatCon Rome conference, DeMuth conducted a long, fawning interview with Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán. Orbán explained how he worked to "get the power and keep the power." DeMuth showed no curiosity about the institutional manipulations involved. The most chilling moment: DeMuth asked Orbán about the "immigration crisis of 2015" (the Syrian refugee crisis), and Orbán responded by gesturing zero — "In Hungary, we have *this* number of Muslim migrants" — to audience applause.
