# Sam Francis

**Paleoconservative political theorist and journalist (1947–2005); *Chronicles* editor; coined "Middle American Radicals" (MARs) as the sociological base for right-wing populism; author of "Beautiful Losers" (1993); fired from the *Washington Times* in 1995 for speech at Jared Taylor's American Renaissance conference endorsing racial separatism; direct intellectual precursor to Trumpism**

Francis's career tracked paleoconservatism's journey from dissident conservative intellectualism to the Hard Right's white nationalist fringe. He was a Heritage Foundation policy analyst in the 1980s before his hard-right turn.

Key concepts:
- **Middle American Radicals (MARs)**: His sociological label for the white working and lower-middle class who felt abandoned by both parties — the cosmopolitan elites above them and the welfare-dependent poor below. Trump's 2016 coalition matches MARs almost exactly; Steve Bannon absorbed the framework and used it explicitly.
- **"Beautiful Losers"** (1993, *Chronicles*): Argued that the conservative movement always loses because it defends universal principles (property rights, rule of law, market freedom) rather than the *particular* civilization — white, Christian, Western — that produced those principles. Movement conservatism was, on this account, designed to lose gracefully while appearing to fight.
- ***Leviathan and Its Enemies*** (posthumous, 2016): Drawing on James Burnham's managerial-revolution thesis, Francis argued that the modern state and corporate capitalism had fused into a managerial elite hostile to Middle American values — a framework that anticipates the New Right's attack on the "administrative state."

Joshua Tait's "Conservatives or Counterrevolutionaries?" treats Francis as a key data point for the counterrevolutionary thesis: Francis dropped the pretense of Burkean conservatism entirely and advocated explicitly for what Tait calls the counterrevolutionary tradition.
