# Josh Hammer

**Duke University BA; University of Chicago Law School JD; editor at *Newsweek*; Edmund Burke Foundation research fellow; early NatCon enthusiast; author of "Common Good Originalism" and related constitutional theory; perhaps the most prolific popularizer of common-good constitutional thinking after Vermeule**

---

## Who He Is

Josh Hammer is a conservative lawyer and journalist who has served as editor at *Newsweek* and as a research fellow with the **Edmund Burke Foundation** (the organization behind the National Conservatism conferences). He is one of the most industrious writers in the New Right ecosystem, producing a steady stream of articles, essays, and "salvos" promoting a constitutional conservatism that takes its cue from Vermeule while trying to situate it within the American originalist tradition.

Unlike Vermeule — whose approach is "continental" and abstract — Hammer sought to root his framework in the Aristotelian and Straussian tradition of Jaffa and the natural law theory of **Hadley Arkes**. He sometimes called his outlook "moral originalism" or "Manly Originalism."

---

## The *Bostock* Catalyst

Hammer (like Vermeule) was deeply offended by Justice **Neil Gorsuch**'s ruling in ***Bostock v. Clayton County, Georgia*** (2020), in which the Supreme Court ruled that federal anti-discrimination laws protecting against sex discrimination also protect LGBTQ employees. This was precisely the outcome conservative legal originalism was supposed to prevent — but Gorsuch, a Federalist Society–vetted "originalist golden boy," reached it through textualist reasoning.

Hammer's reaction, echoing Josh Hawley:

> "There is no escaping the takeaway of *Bostock v. Clayton County, Georgia*, in which Federalist Society-vetted 'originalist' golden boy Neil Gorsuch became the latest member of the ignominious list of Republican nominees at the Court to cave on a civilization-defining cultural issue. That conclusion is both stark and depressing: The conservative legal movement, with all its attendant institutions, theories, and pedagogies, *has failed conservatism*."

*Bostock* confirmed for Hammer — and for much of the New Right — that originalism as a methodology was insufficient. What was needed was a more explicitly moralized jurisprudence.

---

## Common Good Originalism and Related Work

Shortly after Vermeule's original *Atlantic* article (2020), Hammer raised a tweaked, less radical alternative in the Claremont Institute's *American Mind* — an article called "Common Good Originalism." He went on to produce a series of articles and "salvos," including:

- "Undoing the Court's Supreme Transgression"
- "Who's Afraid of the Common Good?"
- "A Better Originalism"
- "The Telos of the American Regime"

In the summer of 2021, he published a longer essay in the **Harvard Journal of Law & Public Policy** (which describes itself as "the nation's leading forum for conservative and libertarian legal scholarship"), claiming that "the state of conservative jurisprudence in America has reached a crisis point." He endorsed "Professor Vermeule's belief that solipsistic citizens' 'own perceptions of what is best for them' are, for all intents and purposes, constitutionally irrelevant" — essentially signing on to Vermeule's demotion of individual liberty in the American constitutional pantheon.

---

## Popularization and Networks

Hammer helped keep the New Right constitutional movement active and growing. Roger Kimball's cultural journal *The New Criterion* published a special symposium on the theme in January 2022. Hammer also explicitly emphasized "the connection between common-good originalism and national conservatism, which is more willing to prudentially wield state power to pursue a substantive vision of the good."

He accused former Heritage Foundation vice president **Kim R. Holmes** of "not knowing what time it is in America" — the same charge Field uses against Robert P. George. He expressed hope that Heritage president **Kevin Roberts** would have "a shrewder understanding of what time it is in these late-stage United States."

---

## Field's Assessment

Hammer represents the **popularizing layer** of the common-good constitutional movement: translating Vermeule's dense legal theory into more accessible, Claremont-adjacent language for the broader New Right. His invocation of Jaffa and Aristotle allowed readers already steeped in West Coast Straussianism to adopt common-good thinking without fully committing to the Schmittian or integralist framework underlying Vermeule's work.
