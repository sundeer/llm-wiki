# Wilson Carey McWilliams (1933–2005)

**Rutgers political theorist; communitarian; author of *The Idea of Fraternity in America* (1973), a 700-page study of the non-liberal "counter-tradition" of fraternity, friendship, and citizenship in American political thought; left-leaning in his own politics; the dominant intellectual influence on the undergraduate and graduate [[Patrick Deneen]] (Rutgers BA 1986, PhD).** Son of the leftist journalist Carey McWilliams (interrogated by HUAC).

McWilliams is significant for the wiki only insofar as Deneen's intellectual formation depends on him. The communitarian counter-tradition McWilliams built his career describing — emphasizing solidarity, shared norms, religious and ethnic subcultures, and the limits of liberal individualism — is the ground from which Deneen later launches his postliberal critique. McWilliams himself, however, was a left-democrat; his daughter (Susan McWilliams Barndt, who studied under Deneen at Princeton) has publicly worried that Deneen's recent trajectory loses sight of an essential McWilliams commitment: that "while there were limitations to the desirability of the liberal order, there were alternatives that are much more frightening."

McWilliams died of a heart attack in March 2005, the same year Deneen left Princeton (after being denied tenure) for Georgetown. Deneen's friend Joseph Romance: "When Carey died, he lost something that centered him." The wiki treats McWilliams's death as one inflection point in Deneen's drift from left-communitarianism toward Catholic postliberalism.
