# Jackie Robinson (1919–1972)

**Hall of Fame baseball player; the first African-American to play in modern Major League Baseball (Brooklyn Dodgers, 1947); civil-rights activist and lifelong Republican until the [[1964 Republican National Convention]] forced him out of the party; the wiki's principal example of *Black Republican* opposition to the conservative-movement capture of the GOP in 1964.**

---

## Why He Matters Here

Robinson was a public-figure Republican who actively campaigned for Nixon in 1960 and *was forced out by [[Barry Goldwater]]'s nomination in 1964*. His syndicated newspaper column and his post-1964 organizing (Republicans and Independents for Lyndon Johnson) supply the wiki's clearest contemporary record of how the conservative-movement takeover of the GOP looked to its remaining Black members.

Three Robinson statements are wiki-load-bearing:

- **August 1963**, anticipating the Goldwater bid: "The danger of the Republican party being taken over by the lily-white-ist conservatives is more serious than many people realize."
- **July 1964**, after the convention: "I now believe I know how it felt to be a Jew in Hitler's Germany."
- **Post-1964**, the structural diagnosis: "If Goldwater has been defeated, but Goldwaterism remains triumphant in GOP councils, America faces a difficult future. We must have a two-party system. The Negro needs to be able to occupy a bargaining position."

Robinson's structural diagnosis — the *figure* loses but the *politics* metastasize inside the party — is, six decades early, the [[Counterrevolution]] thesis the wiki uses to read post-2016 conservatism.

## Career-Adjacent Notes

- 1947: broke the MLB color line with the Brooklyn Dodgers.
- 1960: campaigned for Richard Nixon.
- 1964: backed Rockefeller and Scranton in the GOP primaries, then organized Republicans and Independents for Lyndon Johnson.
- His syndicated *Chicago Defender* column documented the reaction inside Black Republican circles to the convention's racism (the William-Young-suit-set-on-fire incident; the George-Fleming-self-respect statement; the verbal abuse of Black delegates) — the contemporaneous record [[Matthew Delmont]] cites in [[How Jackie Robinson Helped Defeat a Trump-Like Candidate]].
