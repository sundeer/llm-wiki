# Angelo Codevilla (1943–2021)

**Italian-born American political scientist; Boston University international relations professor (1991–2012); senior fellow at Claremont Institute; author of "America's Ruling Class — and the Perils of Revolution" (*American Spectator*, July 2010); the wiki's named *intellectual antecedent* of [[Michael Anton|Anton]]'s [[The Flight 93 Election]] (2016) and the original popularizer of the "ruling class vs. country class" frame [[Rush Limbaugh|Limbaugh]] then carried into mass conservative discourse.**

---

## Why He Matters Here

Per [[The Shaming of the Never Trumpers|Limbaugh's September 7, 2016 broadcast]], Limbaugh explicitly named Codevilla's *American Spectator* essay as "on a par" with Anton's *Flight 93* — and structurally as the *prior* text. The Codevilla → Limbaugh → Anton → Trump-base lineage is the wiki's most economical chain of intellectual transmission for the populist-conservative argument of 2016:

- **Codevilla (2010, *American Spectator*)** — "America's Ruling Class": elite-vs-country-class structural framing
- **Limbaugh (2010-onward)** — popularization on talk radio
- **Anton (2016, *Claremont Review of Books*)** — catastrophist culmination as [[The Flight 93 Election]]
- **Trump (2016-onward)** — political incarnation

Codevilla's contribution was the *naming* of the binary. The piece argued that American politics was fundamentally a class struggle between the *Ruling Class* (educated, credentialed, government-and-academy-aligned, cosmopolitan) and the *Country Class* (everyone else). The frame was a permission structure for treating the establishment-vs-populist axis as the *primary* American political cleavage — not left vs. right.

Codevilla's structural argument is older Burnhamite ([[James Burnham|Burnham]]'s managerial-class thesis recoded for the post-Cold-War context) but in distinctly populist register. Codevilla was a Claremont senior fellow, and the line from his essay to the contemporary [[Claremont Institute]] is direct.
