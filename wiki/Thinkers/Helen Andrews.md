# Helen Andrews

**American conservative essayist and editor; senior editor at *The American Conservative*; author of *Boomers: The Men and Women Who Promised Freedom and Delivered Disaster* (2021); the wiki's named contributor to the contemporary [[António Salazar|Salazar]] revival via her 2021 [[First Things|*First Things*]] essay favorably reviewing Tom Gallagher's *Salazar: The Dictator Who Refused to Die* and proposing Iberian-corporatism as a model for contemporary American politics.**

---

## Why She Matters Here

Per [[Iberian Vistas]], Andrews's 2021 *First Things* review of Gallagher delivered the cleanest articulation of the *corporatist* case for Salazar's regime in mainstream-conservative venue:

> "American society is more and more managed 'pre-politically' by various elites — bureaucrats, tech companies, NGOs. The difference is that our corporative actors answer to no higher authority. The Portuguese had Salazar single-handedly mediating among society's various interests. They also had a shared idea of the common good and a Catholic moral vocabulary to which competing parties could appeal. Without these external constraints, rule by elites is not corporatism but oligarchy."

The argument's structure is wiki-significant: Andrews concedes that contemporary America already has corporative actors (bureaucracies, tech companies, NGOs) functioning as quasi-political authorities, and argues that what the *Estado Novo* added — a *single sovereign authority* mediating among them — is precisely what would convert oligarchy into legitimate corporatism. This is, in mainstream-conservative idiom, the case for an [[Adrian Vermeule|Vermeule]]-style executive consolidation of authority.

Andrews's *Boomers* (2021) — biographical-essay treatment of figures including Steve Jobs, Aaron Sorkin, Camille Paglia, and Sonia Sotomayor — is also wiki-adjacent for its sharply anti-Boomer-liberal generational politics, which intersects the [[Tucker Carlson|Carlson]] register.
