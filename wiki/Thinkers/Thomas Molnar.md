# Thomas Molnar (1921–2010)

**Hungarian-born American Catholic philosopher; Brooklyn College emeritus; longtime *National Review* contributor; traditionalist Catholic theorist; author of *The Counter-Revolution* (1969), *Authority and Its Enemies* (1976), and *Twin Powers: Politics and the Sacred* (1988); a parallel figure to [[Erik von Kuehnelt-Leddihn]] inside the postwar [[National Review|*NR*]] circle's continental-Catholic register; defender of Portuguese colonialism in *NR* during the decolonization era.**

---

## Why He Matters Here

Per [[Iberian Vistas]], Molnar (alongside Kuehnelt-Leddihn) was one of the *NR*'s European-correspondent voices defending Salazar's Portuguese colonial project in Africa as civilization-defense:

> "[The civilizing force of Western colonialism manifested] a moral vigor, [and] an undivided loyalty to a cause… In the western world, as far as I can see, there are only three such nations possessing this stuff: the Americans, the Portuguese, and the South Africans." (1966)

Molnar's grouping of the U.S. with Portugal and apartheid-era South Africa is the wiki's cleanest example of the *anti-decolonization-as-civilization-defense* logic that ran through the postwar Catholic *NR* circle.

Molnar's later book *The Counter-Revolution* (1969) is — as Tait's [[Conservatives or Counterrevolutionaries?]] effectively notes — an early explicit articulation of the counterrevolutionary self-understanding the wiki tracks as central to the [[Counterrevolution]] frame. Molnar argued that "counter-revolution" was not a reactionary refusal but a *positive* substantive politics aimed at restoring proper hierarchical order. The argument is structurally close to the contemporary [[Postliberalism|postliberal]] position, sixty years early.
