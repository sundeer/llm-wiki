# Mike Johnson

**Republican Congressman from Louisiana (LA-4); elected Speaker of the House on October 25, 2023 — third in line to the presidency; Southern Baptist; combines Christian Founding Exceptionalism (CFE) theology with Independent Charismatic/NAR network contacts; participated in Jim Garlow's World Prayer Network call (October 2, 2023) three weeks before becoming Speaker**

---

## Who He Is

Mike Johnson (born 1972, Shreveport, Louisiana) is a Southern Baptist congressman who rose to the speakership in a chaotic post-McCarthy vacancy. He was elected on October 25, 2023, on the fourth vote after a weeks-long Republican internal crisis that had eliminated Kevin McCarthy, Jim Jordan, and Tom Emmer. His election was understood by Christian nationalist networks as a divine appointment.

Field presents Johnson as the culmination of Chapter 12's argument: by late 2023, a figure with explicit Christian nationalist theology — combining CFE claims about America's founding with charismatic networks prophesying divine intervention in politics — had reached the third position in the constitutional line of succession.

---

## World Prayer Network Call (October 2, 2023)

Three weeks before his elevation to the speakership, Johnson participated in a **World Prayer Network** call organized by **Jim Garlow** — an evangelical pastor and Christian nationalist organizer with connections to both CFE circles and the Independent Charismatic network. On the call, Johnson articulated:

- America as "a nation subservient to Him" (God)
- A theology of divine "shaking" — the charismatic/NAR doctrine that God disrupts political circumstances through dramatic interventions
- The belief that America has a special covenantal relationship with God, requiring governance in accordance with Christian principles

The call was made during the speakership vacuum. Johnson's emergence as Speaker was subsequently framed by many in the charismatic network as prophetic fulfillment.

---

## Theology

Johnson's theology synthesizes several strands:

**Christian Founding Exceptionalism**: Johnson has consistently argued that America's founding documents reflect Christian principles and that the nation is in covenant with God. He is associated with the Hillsdale/ISI version of CFE, though his own formation is through evangelical Baptist networks.

**Independent Charismatic adjacency**: The World Prayer Network call and his "shaking" language connect him to NAR-adjacent networks even though he is not himself a charismatic. The language of divine disruption, prophetic fulfillment, and America as a "city on a hill" in covenant with God bridges CFE and charismatic Christianity.

**Anti-secularism**: Johnson has been explicit that the "separation of church and state" is a "lie of the left" — a position that aligns him with [[Christian Nationalism|Christian nationalist]] jurisprudence rejecting Establishment Clause precedents.

---

## Political Career

Johnson served in the Louisiana state legislature before being elected to Congress in 2016. He was involved in:
- **Election denial**: one of the most active congressional supporters of challenges to the 2020 election results; signed onto Texas v. Pennsylvania (the Supreme Court case attempting to invalidate swing-state electoral votes)
- **Anti-LGBTQ legislation**: authored Louisiana's ban on same-sex marriage (later superseded by *Obergefell*) and has been one of the most consistent anti-LGBTQ voices in Congress
- **January 6 adjacent**: his legal theories about state legislatures having authority to decertify electoral results were closely related to the [[John Eastman]] memos

## Significance

Field's framing: Johnson's speakership represents the moment at which the intellectual project traced in *Furious Minds* — the convergence of Christian nationalism, postliberalism, the Claremonters, and Trumpism — achieved its most direct executive power. The man who had argued for overturning the 2020 election results was now two heartbeats from the presidency.

Christopher Hitchens's formula for Straussianism — "Straussians believe in religion and not in God" — applies only to the secular Claremonters. Johnson genuinely believes. That sincerity is, in Field's view, at once more honest and more dangerous than the instrumental religion of the Hillsdale faculty.
