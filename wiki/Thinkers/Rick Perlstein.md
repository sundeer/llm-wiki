# Rick Perlstein (b. 1969)

**American historian and journalist; author of the canonical four-book sequence on the rise of postwar American conservatism — *Before the Storm* (Goldwater, 2001), *Nixonland* (2008), *The Invisible Bridge* (Reagan to 1976, 2014), and *Reaganland* (1976–1980, 2020). For the first two decades of the twenty-first century the most widely read narrative historian of the American right; one of the principal authors of the "respectable rise of conservatism" consensus that Trump's 2016 victory exposed as significantly too narrow.**

Perlstein matters here for two reasons:

1. **He built the standard story.** The wiki's [[Counterrevolution]] thesis is in significant tension with the "Buckley excommunicated the Birchers; Goldwater carried the cleansed movement to mainstream legitimacy; Reagan synthesized it" narrative — which Perlstein's *Before the Storm* (2001) helped make the academic consensus. When *Perlstein himself* concedes the consensus was wrong, that concession carries unusual weight.

2. **He named the failure publicly and early.** [[I Thought I Understood the American Right]] (NYT Magazine, April 2017) is Perlstein's self-reckoning four months into the Trump presidency. He admits the standard narrative was systematically too narrow — it excluded the 1920s Klan's mainstream presence, the 1930s Christian Front "small pogroms" against Jews in NYC and Boston, the Black Legion, the *herrenvolk*-republican strain of American politics, and the lineage of "con-artistry" running from Viguerie's direct mail to Trump's MLM scams. He cites Leo Ribuffo's diagnosis: historians, mostly liberal, were attracted to "polite" subjects like William F. Buckley.

Perlstein's piece is concordant with [[Matthew Dallek]]'s *Birchers* (2023, [[Debunking a Longstanding Myth About William F. Buckley]]) and [[Joshua Tait]]'s [[Conservatives or Counterrevolutionaries?]] (2024). Three different methodological approaches converging on a single revisionist finding about American conservatism's continuity with its supposedly expelled fringe.

Perlstein is a historian, not a movement figure. The page is preserved as a stub for now; expand if more of his work is ingested.
