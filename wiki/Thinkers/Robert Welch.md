# Robert Welch (1899–1985)

**Retired Massachusetts candy manufacturer; founder and lifetime president of the [[John Birch Society]] (1958); the figure [[William F. Buckley Jr.]] used as a symbolic sacrifice to draw a *cordon sanitaire* around the postwar conservative movement, while leaving the JBS rank-and-file and most of its substantive politics in place.**

---

## Who He Was

Welch made his fortune in the candy business (the James O. Welch Company). After WWII he turned increasingly to anti-communist political activism, publishing the screed *The Politician*, which alleged that President Eisenhower was "a dedicated, conscious agent of the Communist conspiracy." In December 1958, in a two-day presentation to a small group of business executives, he founded the John Birch Society. He named it after U.S. Army Capt. John M. Birch, killed by Chinese communists in August 1945; Welch considered him the Cold War's first casualty.

---

## Conspiracism

Welch's distinctive contribution to American politics was the systematization of conspiracism on the right. Representative claims:

- The U.S. government was "50 to 70 percent" communist-controlled (1961).
- Sen. Robert Taft's cancer "may have been induced by a radium tube planted in the upholstery of his Senate seat."
- Black southerners' civil-rights agitation had "been fomented also entirely by the Communists."
- Fluoridation of municipal water supplies was a communist plot.
- Late-1960s "Insiders" theory generalized the conspiracy beyond communism to a banking and business elite — a move that anticipated later New Right framings of "the Cathedral," globalist elites, and the deep state.

Richard Hofstadter's "The Paranoid Style in American Politics" (1964) used Welch as a primary case study; Bob Dylan mocked the JBS in "Talkin' John Birch Paranoid Blues."

---

## Why He Matters Here

Welch is **the man Buckley sacrificed** in 1961–1965 to keep the substantive Bircher politics inside the conservative tent. Per [[Debunking a Longstanding Myth About William F. Buckley]], Buckley's editorials targeted Welch by name while Buckley wrote privately to Birch leaders that he had not made "a single unfavorable reference to any members of the John Birch Society." Welch's most baroque conspiracism (a radium tube in Taft's Senate chair) made him an easy mark; the underlying ideological substrate (anti–civil rights, anti-UN, anti-institutional, racial demographic anxiety, conspiracism about elite betrayal) survived him.

This is the wiki's [[Counterrevolution]] case study in miniature: discipline the embarrassing personality, keep the politics. The pattern recurs — see [[Charlottesville 2017]] (the New Right disavows Richard Spencer's brand while preserving much of the demographic anxiety), the post-Jan-6 Claremont split, and the post-2024 Heritage absorption of Tucker Carlson and the [[Edmund Burke Foundation]] orbit.
