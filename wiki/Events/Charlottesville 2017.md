# Charlottesville 2017 (Unite the Right)

**"Unite the Right" rally; August 12, 2017; Charlottesville, Virginia; organized in part by Richard Spencer; murder of Heather Heyer by James Alex Fields Jr.; marked both the alt-right's apogee and the beginning of its public collapse; Steve Bannon ousted from White House same month**

---

## What Happened

The "Unite the Right" rally in Charlottesville, Virginia on August 12, 2017 was the culmination of the alt-right's post-Trump-election momentum, and simultaneously its turning point.

**Background**: Richard Spencer had led earlier rallies in Charlottesville in May 2017 to protest the planned removal of a Confederate statue of Robert E. Lee. These featured chants of "Russia is our friend!" and "You will not replace us."

**The rally**: Spencer organized the August rally in part and featured as a speaker and leader of the tiki-torch march. The event faced opposition and counterprotests.

**The murder**: James Alex Fields Jr. of Ohio intentionally drove his car into a crowd of protesters on August 12, 2017, killing Heather Heyer and injuring nineteen others. Fields pleaded guilty in 2019 to twenty-nine federal hate crimes and received a life sentence.

---

## Aftermath

**Steve Bannon**: Ousted from the White House. He had already been scheduled to leave, but Trump's new chief of staff John Kelly insisted he leave immediately after Charlottesville.

**Jason Jorjani**: Spencer's AltRight Corporation co-founder; suspended from his academic job at the New Jersey Institute of Technology after a *New York Times* exposé on his extremism (September 2017); fell out with Spencer; left Arktos.

**Julius Krein**: His New York Times op-ed disavowing Trump — "I Voted for Trump. And I Sorely Regret It" — was published August 17, 2017, five days after Charlottesville.

**Alt-right decline**: "In many respects, Charlottesville marked the decline of the alt-right and the holding to account of some of its key figures." Spencer's public profile was substantially diminished.

---

## The Paradox: Decline and Mainstreaming

Field's key observation: Charlottesville simultaneously marked the alt-right's public decline AND the beginning of a new process — its ideas being mainstreamed through more respectable channels. "Some of its key figures merely migrated to, or invented, other platforms; others worked their ideas into more conventional-seeming outlets. Bannon was out, but Miller would remain in the White House. The 'alt-right' was on the way out, but the 'dissident' Hard Right was rising."

The Claremont Institute subsequently published Michael Anton's sympathetic review of *Bronze Age Mindset*, and Claremont president Ryan Williams went on the *Jack Murphy Live* podcast to explain "why they engaged with Bronze Age Pervert, Curtis Yarvin, and Jack Posobiec."
