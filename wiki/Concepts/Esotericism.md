# Esotericism (Straussian)

**[[Leo Strauss]]'s doctrine that great philosophers wrote in two registers — an exoteric surface teaching for the masses and an esoteric hidden teaching for careful readers — and that recovering the hidden teaching is the central task of political philosophy**

---

## What It Is

Strauss's doctrine of esotericism, developed most fully in *Persecution and the Art of Writing* (1952), holds that throughout history, philosophers faced political danger from expressing heterodox views openly. To protect themselves — and to protect unphilosophical readers from destabilizing truths — philosophers wrote in coded ways: the surface argument is orthodox and acceptable; the real argument emerges only from careful reading of contradictions, silences, and structural anomalies.

Strauss argued this is not merely a historical curiosity but a clue to how philosophy must always operate: the truths of political philosophy are dangerous to political society and must be transmitted carefully.

---

## Implications for Political Philosophy

The esotericist doctrine has several consequences for how Straussians approach texts and politics:

1. **Slow reading**: texts must be read with extreme care for what they do *not* say as much as what they say
2. **Elite transmission**: genuine philosophical knowledge passes through careful readers, not mass education
3. **Philosopher's distance from politics**: Strauss himself was skeptical of philosophers directly entering politics
4. **"Gentleman" intermediary**: between philosopher and populace stands the "gentleman" — not fully philosophical but shaped by philosophy; educated elites who can translate philosophical insight into politically viable forms

---

## Esotericism in the New Right Context

The Straussian esotericist tradition has an ambiguous relationship to the New Right. Strauss himself was politically cautious and did not endorse populism. But his students divided:

**East Coast Straussians** maintained the scholarly, detached posture Strauss modeled.

**West Coast Straussians** ([[Harry V. Jaffa]], [[Michael Anton]], [[Claremont Institute]]) took a more activist stance — willing to write political tracts, to descend into the political arena, to write *pseudonymously* (Anton as "Publius Decius Mus" for [[The Flight 93 Election]]).

Anton's pseudonymous writing is itself a kind of esotericism in reverse: the esoteric author conceals his identity to write for the masses. It is Straussianism turned inside out.

**[[Curtis Yarvin]]** ("Mencius Moldbug") employs a more explicit esotericism: his deliberately dense, elliptical prose is written to attract and select careful readers who can follow the argument to its anti-democratic conclusions.

---

## Critical Perspective

[[Furious Minds|Field]] suggests that the Straussian emphasis on hidden truths and careful readers creates an intellectual culture prone to:
- Self-congratulatory "deep reading" that finds sophisticated meanings wherever convenient
- Elite condescension toward democratic publics
- Justification for deceptive rhetoric ("noble lies") in politics
- A tendency to treat philosophical sophistication as a license for political extremism

The esotericist framework, in other words, provides an intellectual culture that is structurally hospitable to the kind of cynical political manipulation the New Right has normalized.
