# The Cathedral

**[[Curtis Yarvin]]'s concept for the informal network of universities, mainstream media, NGOs, and government bureaucracies that, in his view, collectively enforce progressive ideological orthodoxy without central coordination; the primary structural target of neoreactionary and New Right critique**

"The Cathedral" is Yarvin's coinage for what he sees as the dominant ideological machine of contemporary American society. Unlike an explicit propaganda ministry, The Cathedral operates through dispersed networks of credentialed professionals — academics, journalists, foundation officers, policy experts — who share a common secular-progressive worldview absorbed through elite education. The result, Yarvin argues, is that elite consensus functions as a de facto state religion, enforcing orthodoxy through prestige and exclusion rather than coercion. Hence the ecclesiastical metaphor: authority without formal command.

The concept circulates well beyond Yarvin's neoreactionary niche. In mainstream right-wing discourse, "The Cathedral" operates as a more intellectually elevated synonym for "the establishment," "the blob," "the deep state," or "the mainstream media." [[Steve Bannon]]'s framing of Breitbart as "the platform for the alt-right" and his institutional war on "legacy media" draws on the same structural intuition.

Field documents The Cathedral's influence on [[Christopher Rufo]]'s [[Longmarcherism]]: Rufo's "long march through the institutions" is explicitly a counter-Cathedral strategy — infiltrating the same nodes (universities, media, K-12 education, federal bureaucracy) and converting them. The tactical logic is the same; the ideology is inverted.

The concept also shapes how the [[Hard Right]] understands the Claremont Institute's [[Ideas First]] theory: if The Cathedral is the mechanism of left intellectual hegemony, a counter-Cathedral is required — an interlocking network of think tanks, journals, fellowships, and media that produces and distributes a competing orthodoxy.
