# Decisionism

**[[Carl Schmitt]]'s political theory holding that sovereignty consists in the power to decide the exception — to declare a state of emergency and suspend normal legal order; widely adopted by the New Right as a theoretical resource for authoritarian executive power**

---

## What It Is

Decisionism is Schmitt's answer to the question "what is sovereignty?" His answer: "Sovereign is he who decides on the state of exception." (*Politische Theologie*, 1922)

The key move is the concept of the *Ausnahmezustand* — the state of exception, emergency, or exception. Normal legal order depends on a prior decision about what counts as normal; in a true crisis, someone must decide to suspend the rules. That decider is the sovereign. Parliamentary procedure and constitutional norms presuppose a stable order; when that order is genuinely threatened, decision comes before procedure.

This is not just an empirical observation but a normative claim: Schmitt argues that liberal constitutionalism's pretense of rule-of-law neutrality is a *fiction* — in genuine crises, someone always decides, and pretending otherwise is naive or dishonest.

---

## Schmitt's Friend/Enemy Distinction

Closely related to decisionism is Schmitt's friend/enemy distinction (from *The Concept of the Political*, 1932): the defining characteristic of "the political" is the distinction between friend and enemy. Politics is not about interests or values but about collective identities in potential conflict. A political community constitutes itself by identifying who it is *against*.

This framework is deeply anti-liberal: liberalism tries to dissolve the friend/enemy distinction into economic competition and moral debate, but Schmitt argues this merely masks political reality without changing it. The political always returns.

## Decisionism in the New Right

The New Right's appropriation of Schmitt operates on multiple levels:

**Practical-political**: [[The Fight is Now]] and [[The Flight 93 Election]] both deploy decisionist logic — the claim that the normal rules don't apply because a genuine state of emergency exists. America faces existential destruction; the time for constitutional procedure is over; someone must decide.

**Theoretical**: [[Adrian Vermeule]]'s [[Integration from Within]] and [[Common Good Constitutionalism]] draw on Schmitt's critique of liberal neutrality. The pretense that law is neutral between competing visions of the good is, Vermeule agrees with Schmitt, a liberal ideology masquerading as procedure.

**Hard Right**: [[Curtis Yarvin]] and the Bronze Age Pervert draw more directly on Schmittian decisionism in their advocacy for techno-monarchy and authoritarian leadership — the "Caesar" who cuts through parliamentary paralysis.

---

## The Schmitt Problem

[[Furious Minds|Field]] identifies what she calls "the Schmitt problem" as one of the New Right's central intellectual tensions: you cannot cleanly separate Schmitt's political theory from his biography (Nazi jurist, 1933 NSDAP member) or from the uses to which his framework lends itself. Decisionism, in practice, means: whoever has power can declare the emergency that justifies breaking the rules.

The New Right thinkers who use Schmitt — Vermeule, Deneen, Ahmari — typically do not reckon fully with this problem. They borrow the critique of liberalism without confronting what follows when the "decider" is not your preferred philosopher-king but an authoritarian who has captured the state of exception for his own ends.
