# Longmarcherism

**New Right ideological framework, articulated ~2021; claims radical leftists deliberately infiltrated and conquered American institutions over decades via a "long march"; used to justify the New Right's own institutional assault as a defensive "counter-revolution"; most fully developed by Christopher Rufo in *America's Cultural Revolution* (2023)**

---

## What It Is

Longmarcherism is Field's term for a new "galaxy-brained theory" that the New Right elaborated beginning around 2021 to justify radical institutional action. The basic story:

> For decades, radical leftists had been engaged in a deliberate, top-down radicalization scheme to infiltrate and conquer American institutions. They had succeeded, so it was time to mount a counterattack and siege.

It is, Field notes, "a fresh variation on a familiar 'hour is late' refrain" — the emergency logic that has justified New Right escalation at every stage, from the Flight 93 election (2016) through January 6 (2021) to institutional assault (2021–present).

---

## The Historical Source: Herbert Marcuse

The theory traces the left's strategy to **Herbert Marcuse** (1898–1979), a German-American philosopher of the New Left — and, notably, the dissertation supervisor of Paul Gottfried (the paleo-conservative who coined "alt-right"). Marcuse described a "long march through the institutions" as "working against the established institutions while working from within them." He also recommended building "counterinstitutions" or "counter-cadres" in nongovernmental arenas like media and education.

Rufo et al. took these as a strategic blueprint and argued they described the actual history of the past half-century: that "woke" leftists and Marxists had systematically infiltrated and conquered "the commanding heights of culture" as well as elected government, the administrative state, and the corporate world — "over the course of the prior decades, accelerating under the first Trump administration, and peaking with Biden."

---

## Rufo's *America's Cultural Revolution* (2023)

[[Christopher Rufo]]'s book is the fullest elaboration of the Longmarcher thesis. It traces the left's "takeover" from the 1960s New Left theorists through Black Power, critical pedagogy (Paulo Freire), and queer theory, to DEI bureaucracies in universities and corporations.

Field's verdict: "a yarn, a tall tale, a story." The Longmarcher account simply doesn't match reality:
- The left won real victories (civil rights, *Roe v. Wade*, marriage equality)
- But there were also major losses and clawbacks (mass incarceration, *Citizens United*, the conservative legal movement's 50-year project to recapture the judiciary)
- Wokeness was a *response* to real-world problems (racist police violence, Trumpism) — not an elite imposition from above
- The police were not defunded, prisons were not abolished, reparations went unpaid

**Zack Beauchamp's review**: "Rufo has serially exaggerated the phantom menace of a leftist cultural revolution because he seeks to justify its right-wing mirror: a wholesale assault on mainstream liberal institutions designed to indoctrinate the public into its preferred social vision."

---

## The Strategic Function

What Longmarcherism does, politically, is provide a *defensive* narrative for an *offensive* action. By claiming the institutions have already been conquered by the left, the New Right can frame its own seizure and demolition of those institutions as counter-revolution rather than revolution — restoration rather than radicalism.

Rufo at Hillsdale (2022): "even those of us who are temperamentally predisposed to defense must recognize that offense — laying siege to the institutions — is what is now demanded." The book's concluding promise: "The theorists of the counter-revolution must breathe new life into the American myth, and mobilize the tremendous reservoir of public sentiment toward a project of restoration." The goal is not to assume control of the bureaucracy, "but to smash it."

## Relationship to Other New Right Concepts

Longmarcherism is of a piece with several other New Right frameworks for explaining why elite institutions are illegitimate:
- [[The Cathedral]] (Yarvin): elite institutions as a self-reproducing oligarchy
- **Davoisie**, **liberalocracy**: variations on managerial elite theory
- **Longhouse** (BAP/Alamariu): feminized institutional culture that suppresses masculine virtue
- [[The Flight 93 Election]]: the emergency logic that justified the 2016 election as existential
- **Claremont's "two constitutions"**: elite jurisprudence as constitutional betrayal

What is distinctive about Longmarcherism is its *historical* form — it makes a specific empirical claim about how the takeover happened, which gives it the feel of scholarly analysis while serving the same political function.
