# Administrative State

**The body of unelected federal regulatory agencies, civil-service bureaucracies, and rule-making bodies that the New Right treats as the locus of post-1930s liberal governance; what [[Schedule F]], [[Project 2025]], and Claremont legal theory are designed to dismantle**

The phrase functions as the New Right's all-purpose name for the post–New Deal regulatory and bureaucratic state — the EPA, SEC, IRS, ATF, Department of Education, federal civil service — which conservatives view as effectively a "fourth branch" exercising legislative, executive, and judicial power without democratic accountability. Hostility to the administrative state unifies otherwise divergent New Right factions:

- **Claremont legal theorists** (John Marini, Charles Kesler) frame it as a Wilsonian-progressive subversion of the Constitution's separation of powers
- **Common Good Constitutionalists** ([[Adrian Vermeule]]) accept the administrative state as a vehicle but want it captured for substantive moral ends — see [[Integration from Within]]
- **Project 2025** ([[Heritage Foundation]]) treats dismantling/repurposing it as the primary task of a second Trump term, via [[Schedule F]] and aggressive political appointees

See also: [[Schedule F]], [[Counterrevolution]], [[Project 2025]], [[Common Good Constitutionalism]].
