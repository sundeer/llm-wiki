# MAGA New Right

**The coalition of intellectual movements, politicians, and activists that coalesced around Donald Trump between 2016 and 2024; distinguished from earlier conservatism by its rejection of liberal proceduralism, embrace of cultural counter-revolution, and willingness to use state power to impose conservative social ends; the subject of [[Furious Minds]] (Field, 2025) and the organizing object of this wiki**

For the master historical synthesis, see [[Overview]].

---

## What It Is

"MAGA New Right" is Laura K. Field's term for a distinct political-intellectual formation that emerged from the wreckage of the 2016 Republican primary. It is "new" relative to the Reagan-era [[Fusionism]] it displaced — the Cold War synthesis of libertarianism, social conservatism, and anti-communism. It is "MAGA" because it coalesced around Trump's movement, though its intellectual roots predate 2016.

## Field's Taxonomy

Field divides the New Right into three overlapping intellectual camps plus an underbelly:

1. **[[The Claremonters]]** — West Coast Straussians at the [[Claremont Institute]] and [[Hillsdale College]]; [[Declarationism]]; catastrophism; masculinist-counterrevolutionary aesthetic
2. **[[Postliberalism]]** — Catholic social teaching; rejection of liberal proceduralism; [[Catholic Integralism]]; Deneen, Vermeule, Ahmari
3. **[[National Conservatism]]** — big-tent nationalism organized around the Edmund Burke Foundation; Yoram Hazony; NatCon conferences
4. **[[Hard Right]] underbelly** — white nationalists, neoreactionaries, Manosphere figures; not a separate camp but an extremist tendency within all three

## Defining Features

- Rejection of [[Fusionism]] (the Cold War synthesis the New Right replaced)
- Embrace of executive power and administrative state capture
- Cultural counter-revolution: reversal of social liberalism on gender, race, education, and family
- Economic nationalism: industrial policy, tariffs, skepticism of free trade
- Anti-immigration maximalism
- [[Decisionism|Schmittian decisionism]]: the "exception" that justifies emergency measures
- Catastrophism: the liberal moment as existential crisis (→ [[The Flight 93 Election]])
- "[[Ideas First]]": belief that intellectual transformation precedes political change
