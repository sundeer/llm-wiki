# Ideas First

**The methodological premise of the [[MAGA New Right]]'s intellectual wing: that political movements are driven by ideas, not material interests, and that the path to political transformation runs through changing the ideas of elites and institutions rather than through mass organizing**

---

## What It Is

"Ideas First" describes a cluster of related premises about how political change happens, shared across the New Right's intellectual factions despite their other differences:

1. **Ideas, not interests, drive history**: The Right's defeats have been intellectual defeats — failures to develop and propagate the right ideas — not failures of political strategy or coalition-building
2. **Elites are the target**: It is more important to convert lawyers, academics, journalists, judges, and bureaucrats than to mobilize mass constituencies
3. **Long-term thinking**: Transformation takes decades of institution-building and intellectual work
4. **Infrastructure matters**: Think tanks, journals, fellowships, and conferences are not secondary to political action but are the primary site of political work

---

## Origins and Manifestations

The "Ideas First" approach is articulated most clearly by [[R.R. Reno]] in *Return of the Strong Gods*: the New Right needs to change *what people care about* before it can change what they vote for. The "strong gods" — loyalty, solidarity, transgenerational community — must be rehabilitated against the "gods of weakening" (openness, tolerance, fluidity) that postwar liberalism valorized.

The [[Claremont Institute]]'s model is entirely "Ideas First": it is less interested in policy papers than in forming political leaders and intellectuals through its fellowship programs, shaping the assumptions that future leaders will bring to their work.

[[The Flight 93 Election]] is itself an "Ideas First" document: it aims to *change minds* among conservative elites who had refused to vote for Trump, arguing from political philosophy rather than from policy analysis.

[[Yoram Hazony]] and the Edmund Burke Foundation's NatCon conference series instantiate "Ideas First" at scale: bring together intellectuals, politicians, and activists to develop a shared intellectual framework for National Conservatism.

---

## Tension with Populism

There is an obvious tension between "Ideas First" and democratic populism. If the New Right's political energy comes from mass resentment of elites, why is the intellectual strategy focused on elite persuasion?

[[Furious Minds|Field]] identifies this as one of the New Right's central contradictions. The movement is simultaneously anti-elitist (attacking the "establishment," the "deep state," the credentialed class) and hyper-elitist in its theory of political change (focusing on philosophy, jurisprudence, and institutional capture).

The resolution may be strategic: elite capture of institutions can deliver political outcomes that mass mobilization alone cannot — as the Federalist Society's transformation of the federal judiciary demonstrates.
